House Print

Everything on screen that isn't the table is something the house printed: a ticket, a chip, a card, a board, or paint on the road. The driver license looked like this first. House Print takes its paper, its engraving and its stamp to every menu, HUD piece and dialog, so a screenshot of any of them could only come from this game.

Proposed on 2026-10-05, settled and built in full by 2026-10-06. GAME.md 8.1a holds the rules; this page keeps the pictures: the game as it looks now, the mark, the system it is built from, where it started, and every decision on the way.

  • Printed, not paneledEvery surface is house stock or paper cut to a shape. Nothing is a plain translucent box with round corners.
  • Six shapes and a road lineTicket, chip, card, board, strip and sign, with lane dashes for lines and lane chevrons for focus. New screens are built from these.
  • Engraving at a whisperFine line patterns under everything at 4 to 6 percent, drawn in code like the license's, felt before they are seen.

The mark · GAME.md 8.1

A seal for the house

The game's mark is a seal, engraved the way a banknote's is: a scalloped foil medallion, a ring of ink with the house's fine print running round it, and in its middle the foil chip, the Junction seen from above with the pot a red chip on the crossing. Drawn under 96 pixels across it is the foil chip alone. It is drawn in code, LogoArt, and make logo writes every file below into assets/logo/, each at 128, 512 and 2048 pixels.

On Android

A launcher since Android 8 cuts the icon to a shape of its own: the stock and its waves fill the layer behind, the seal sits in front inside the circle no shape cuts into, and a launcher that tints its icons gets the foil chip in one ink.

Materials

Two papers, one foil

Dark card stock for everything you drive past and click through, cream security paper for the few things that are documents. Gold stays the house color. Every other color already exists in the game and keeps the job it has.

Finishes · questions 31 to 41

A surface to everything

Every surface wears a texture made in code and laid inside its shape, so nothing stretches: each light pixel lightens and each dark one darkens a little, so no color moves. A soft light falls from the top left on everything that wears one, and chips show the clay of a real chip. Crops from the game:

Engraving

The lines under everything

The license looks made because of what sits behind its words. Five patterns carry that everywhere. Each is a set of polylines drawn once into a texture and reused, the way DrawnTexture already serves the hologram, so they cost nothing per frame. Shown here about three times stronger than they would be in the game.

Shapes

Six shapes and a road line

Each shape comes from something on the table or beside the road, and each has one job. A ticket is something you read or press. A strip is something you glance at while driving.

Type

A face with a road in it

Inter was a fine face and the default of half the apps on a phone, which was the problem. Barlow, the face now, was drawn after California's highway signs and license plates. Its condensed cut fits a score and a clock into less width and reads at a glance. It is under the OFL like Inter and has tabular figures; Homemade Apple stays for signatures.

Motion

How it moves

  • Dealt, not fadedA pane slides up 24 px and settles in 0.18 s, the way a ticket comes out of a printer, and leaves the same way.
  • The cursor travelsFocus glides from one control to the next instead of jumping, so a pad player sees where it went. Accessibility can turn it off.
  • A press is a step darkerA pressed button's stock darkens a step under the cursor. The click sound stays as it was.
  • Only the title turnsThe title's beams and the seal's rings turn slowly behind the mark and a few chips drift past. No other engraving moves, so nothing on a pane shimmers while you read it.

Play-tests

What playing it changed

Each stage was played before the next began, and the notes from playing it went back in. The proposal's drawings are where it started; these are the turns it took on the way to the screenshots at the top.

Second play-test

  • Panes always open in the middle of the screen; the pause menu no longer pops up at the top.
  • The cursor glides smoothly from control to control, and Accessibility has a switch to turn it off.
  • The pot's pills fit their chip and die, and burn with fire at match point.
  • Every call has a banner of its own: hazard tape, a hand of cards, a checkered flag, road signs; Last Call is neon.
  • The start lights are a gantry of chips; the Royal Flush tally is little suit cards under the clock.
  • The kill feed's own lines carry a stripe; joker names are centered and keep their kind.
  • The payout slip scrolls on a short screen, and its buttons are printed on the paper.
  • Menus fit very small screens, scrolling where they must; touch controls wear the player's color.
  • The title's beams follow the proposal; Now enrolling is a paper arrow at the Driving School.
  • The school's hints, pointers, tick boxes and test panel are in the house style, a lesson done stamped PASSED.
  • The control hints left the player's block and hang under it.

Finishes

  • Panes laminated, the slip on fine paper, glass over the clock, linen on the cards, clay on the chips, a soft light on all of it.
  • Gold foil brushed but fainter, the HUD's strips scratched, the menus on the heavier felt: each tried both ways in the game first.

Third play-test

  • Each button takes its texture from a place of its own, and the felt shows no lines where it repeats.
  • License plates are plain where they are small and printed in full, Dept. of Karts and screws in the corners, where they are big.
  • The slip's lines shade off where there is more to scroll, right against its rules.
  • Emotes are chips; a streak's fire is narrow flames that fade with the banner.
  • The lobby's ticket is two short lines clear of the count; how to take a seat shows in the corner the next player takes.
  • The trade sign stands above its banner, tilted; the control hints hang from the foot of the player's block on a tab, mirrored for the lower corners.

After the play-tests

  • The driving test's lessons sit on an examiner's clipboard. A slight tilt was tried and taken out again: turned text aliases.
  • Seats, profiles and a host's player list share one seat card. Profiles switch, edit and delete with icons, ask before deleting, and are made without switching to them unless asked.
  • The mark became the seal, with the foil chip alone when small; the icon is the seal on nothing, the splash the seal on its plate, and Android has a launcher icon of its own.
  • The boot splash stands on the title's stock.
Plan

Built in five stages

Each stage landed as its own commits with the tests passing and before and after screenshots, and was played before the next began. GAME.md 8.1a, The house print, is the source of truth, and 8.3's pane and type paragraphs point to it.

  1. Foundation and menus BuiltA HouseTheme built in code and merged into the default theme, so every control and popup picks it up without per-control overrides. A TicketStyle StyleBox draws tickets, perforations and foil frames. An Engraving drawer is shared with LicenseCard. Barlow comes in through Type. The title's buttons, settings and online menus change here.
  2. Panes and dialogs BuiltPause menu, ConfirmPane, NameDialog, FormDialog, PasswordPane, lobby settings and the joker pane, with the road signs.
  3. The HUD BuiltThe corner strip in HudBlock, the pot chips in ScorePills, the dial and nitro cells, the LED clock board, the standings strips in Scoreboard, the kill feed strips, key caps and the item tag.
  4. Overlays BuiltThe respawn pick, the joker hand's cards, the payout slip, the Grand Prix standings, match point and Last Call notices, and the loading lane.
  5. Title, school and touch BuiltThe engraved title and wordmark, the driving school's pages, and the touch controls.
